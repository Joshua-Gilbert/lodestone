Grid-OS on drive C: (the internal hard disk) and on M: (the RAM disk in EMS),
BENCH 1.4 in pcemu (2026-10-08, 0.3.14)
python3 bench/run_bench_hd.py

The disk: the layout of Joshua's CP30104H (its own partition table and
boot sector: 762 x 8 x 39, one 121 MB FAT16 partition, 2 KB clusters),
about 800 KB of files at the start as on the real disk.  BENCH runs from
A: with C: (or M:) current, from the command prompt, with the 1520's EMS.
M: is made at 1 MB (MDISK=1024: 256 KB holds too little for BENCH's
files); pcemu charges 1 us for each EMS page-register IN or OUT.

The drive's timing (pcemu.Ata with timed=True; off in the functional tests):
  3,360 rpm (HDPROBE counted the index pulse on the 1520), 39 sectors a
  track; a seek of d cylinders 4 ms + 0.02 ms x d (0.02 ms a cylinder:
  under 20 ms across the disk; HDPROBE's single reads at cylinders 0, 381
  and 761 took 11, 10 and 46 ms); 0.5 ms a command; 1 us a data word
  moved by the CPU.  Reads stream into the drive's buffer as the sectors
  pass the head; a write ends when its last sector has passed the head.
  Two cases, because the drive's own buffer is unknown (IDENTIFY: buffer
  type 3, 64 sectors, which suggests a read cache):
    no look-ahead   the drive reads only what it is asked for
    look-ahead      it keeps what it read and reads on to the end of
                    that track; a new command ends the look-ahead
  Check against the 1520: HDSCAN reads a track per command.  The model
  gives 547 KB/s (two turns a track: the next track's sector 1 has just
  gone by when the command arrives); the real sweep was 463 KB/s (42 ms a
  track, about 2.35 turns).  So the model is about 15% fast on big
  sequential reads; what the real drive does between tracks (head skew,
  its own overhead) isn't known.

- Next to the floppy (emulator, same BENCH: 79.0 s in all), C:'s total is
  3 to 9 times shorter, depending on the look-ahead.  Big writes (12x),
  small files (8x) and EXE loading (14-23x) gain the most: no 300 rpm, no
  motor, short seeks.
- Without the drive's look-ahead, small sequential reads cost a turn of the
  disk each (512 commands for 256 KB in 512-byte reads: 9.4 s).  On the
  floppy Grid-OS's own cylinder read-ahead hides this; it doesn't cover C:.
  If the real drive has no look-ahead, extending the read-ahead to C:
  would bring those two tests from 9.4 s to well under 1 s.
- M: is a further 3 to 9 times faster than C: overall: 0.18 s to write
  256 KB, 0.11 s to read it, 20 small files in 0.18 s, the 120 KB program
  loaded and run 3 times in 0.16 s.
- The 1520 decides which C: column is right: BENCH on C: (C:, then A:BENCH)
  writes C:\BENCH.TXT.

- The CP30104H reads ahead: 512-byte reads take 0.71 s (the look-ahead
  model: 0.96 s; without look-ahead it would be 9.4 s).  Grid-OS's
  read-ahead doesn't need to cover C:.
- The look-ahead column is close: 8.55 s modelled, 9.11 s real.  The
  drive's writes and small files run a little slower than modelled
  (0.87 / 3.57 s against 0.74 / 2.71 s), its reads faster.
- The emulator charges the kernel's own work about twice what the 1520
  takes (INT 21h x 1000: 0.108 s against 0.05 s; the same on the floppy),
  so M:, which is nearly all kernel work, is 2.87 s there and 2.08 s for
  real.
- BENCH times with the BIOS clock: under about 0.2 s, a figure is only
  good to a tick (55 ms).
